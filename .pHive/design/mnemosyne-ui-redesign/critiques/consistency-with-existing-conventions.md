# Consistency with Existing Conventions — Review

**Lens only.** This review judges each mockup solely against the conventions already established in the live app (`ui/index.html`, `ui/style.css`): the flat `<section class="panel">` → `<h2>` → `.panel-status` → content shape repeated eight times in document order; monospace (`ui-monospace, SFMono-Regular, Menlo, monospace`) reserved for tabular/identifier/code data (`.detail`, `.fields dd`, `#personas-table td:nth-child(1/2)`); and exactly one existing progressive-disclosure idiom, a native `<details id="personas-glossary">` used for an optional glossary, nested *inside* the Personas panel, not as page structure. No sidebar, no page-level collapse, no single-active-panel switching, and no page-level sticky nav exist in the live shell today — the only `position: sticky` in the current CSS is a sticky *table* group-header (`.persona-group-header th`), a small in-panel affordance, not a navigation chrome element.

I read all three HTML sources in full and the live `ui/index.html`/`ui/style.css` as the ground truth for "existing conventions," then cross-checked each screenshot.

---

## collapsible-clusters

**What it does structurally:** wraps the 8 existing panels inside three new `<details class="cluster">` elements (System / Memory / Personas), each opened by default, plus a new sticky `#cluster-nav` anchor-link bar above everything.

**Findings:**
- **Extends the real precedent correctly at the macro level.** The one native-`<details>` idiom that already exists in this app (`#personas-glossary`) is genuinely reused here — clusters are native `<details>/<summary>`, not a bespoke JS accordion, no ARIA reinvention. This is the most direct, literal extension of an established idiom of the three mockups.
- **But it breaks the per-panel shape it's nested inside.** In the live app every panel's title is an `<h2>` directly inside `<section class="panel">`. Here, panel titles are demoted to `<h3 class="panel-title">` (e.g. `<h3 class="panel-title">Liveliness</h3>`) because the `<summary>` now occupies the "this is a title" visual role instead. That's a real, needless heading-level regression relative to the live markup, not a cosmetic wash.
- **The Personas panel loses its own heading entirely.** `<section id="personas" class="panel panel-wide">` here has no `<h3 class="panel-title">Personas</h3>` at all — it jumps straight from the section tag to `<p class="panel-status pass">9 personas…</p>`. The panel now depends on the ancestor `<summary>Personas</summary>` for its identity. That's a structural regression against the very shape this lens is supposed to protect (every panel self-identifies via its own heading) and it's inconsistent even within this one mockup (System's `#operations` and `#liveliness` keep their `<h3>`, Personas does not).
- **Introduces a second, unrelated navigational vocabulary on top of the reused one.** `#cluster-nav` (a horizontal pill/link bar, sticky, sub-header) has zero precedent anywhere in the live CSS — no page-level sticky nav exists today. Combined with the new `.cluster`/`.cluster-body`/`.cluster-tagline` classes, this mockup is running two structural systems at once (accordion clusters + jump nav) where the live app runs zero.
- Monospace discipline is otherwise well preserved: `.detail`, `.fields dd`, `td.mono`/`th.mono` all correctly restrict monospace to identifiers/code/tabular data, matching the live convention.
- Visually (per screenshot), the nested-card-inside-a-card look (rounded `.cluster` boxes containing rounded `.panel` boxes with double borders) reads as a different, more "documentation portal" or "settings app" product than the flat panel-stack list the live app currently is — cards-within-cards is not a shape the current app has anywhere.

## sidebar-glanceable

**What it does structurally:** persistent left `<nav id="panel-nav">` (240px, full height) listing all 8 panels with colored pill **status badges**, JS toggles `.panel.active` so only one panel's content is ever visible in `<main>` at a time.

**Findings:**
- **Keeps the per-panel shape intact where it survives.** Every visible panel still uses `<h2>` + `.panel-status` inside `<section class="panel">`, matching the live app's markup almost verbatim (e.g. `<section id="liveliness" class="panel active"><h2>Liveliness</h2><p class="panel-status pass">pass</p>...`). Of the three, this is the cleanest 1:1 reuse of the *internal* panel shape.
- **But the containing interaction model is foreign to this app.** The live shell has never hidden any panel from any other — all 8 render simultaneously, top to bottom, always. `display: none` on 7 of 8 sections plus a single-active-panel switcher is a SPA-settings-page pattern (think a preferences dialog with a left rail) that this codebase's "everything is visible, refresh once, no polling, no route state" philosophy (per `ui/app.js`'s own top comment) does not currently express anywhere. A first-time viewer glancing at this screenshot could reasonably mistake it for a different kind of product (a settings/admin console) rather than the flat operator dashboard Mnemosyne currently is.
- **Invents a new status-badge visual language with no precedent.** The live app's only status idiom is `.panel-status.pass/.fail/.loading` — colored *text*, no chrome. `style.css` has no badge, pill, or dot-indicator class anywhere. This mockup's `.status-badge` (rounded pill, `::before` colored dot, border, uppercase micro-label) sitting in the sidebar is a second, parallel status vocabulary that doesn't exist today, layered on top of (not replacing) the original in-panel `.panel-status` text which is still also present per-panel. Now there are two different ways status is communicated for the same data.
- Table/monospace usage is otherwise faithful to the live convention (`.mono` on lane/collection/score cells, `.fields dd` monospace for settings values, etc.).
- Net effect: internally each panel is the most faithful reproduction of the existing panel shape, but the shell around those panels — persistent nav rail + hide/show + badge system — is the largest structural invention of the three, and the one most likely to read as a different product than the mockup's siblings or the live app.

## minimal-jump-chips

**What it does structurally:** no collapsing, no hiding, no clustering — all 8 panels stay exactly where they are today, in the same order, all always visible. Adds a slim `<nav id="jump-chips">` of pill-shaped anchor links (sticky under the header) purely for scroll-to, plus a small monospace "eyebrow" index number (`01`, `02`, …) inside each `<h2>` and a `border-bottom` divider under each panel title.

**Findings:**
- **Preserves the panel shape exactly, unmodified.** Every section is still `<section id="..." class="panel[...]">` → `<h2>` (untouched, not demoted) → `.panel-status` → content, byte-for-byte the same nesting as the live `ui/index.html`, down to reusing the same panel IDs, the same `.panel-hint`, `.fields`, `.detail`, `.op-block`, `#persona-layer-stack`, `#persona-level0-pointer` substructure, and even the same code comments' worth of copy for Operations/Personas. Nothing here required inventing a new containment model.
- **The one progressive-disclosure element present (`#personas-glossary`) is untouched** — same native `<details>/<summary>`, same position (inside the Personas panel, not promoted to page structure), matching the live app's actual usage exactly rather than generalizing it into something new.
- **The added nav is the smallest and most self-contained departure of the three.** `#jump-chips` is additive chrome only — it doesn't touch how panels render, nest, or reveal themselves; removing it would leave the document identical to today's live app. It's a genuinely new element (no precedent), but it's minimal and doesn't compete with or duplicate an existing status/heading system.
- **The `.eyebrow` index numbers and `border-bottom` divider under each `<h2>` are a mild, additive typographic change**, not a new component — they ride inside the existing `<h2>`, don't introduce a new class of status/nav widget, and don't change monospace scope (the eyebrow number is monospace, arguably a defensible micro-use of the existing "monospace = identifiers/data" rule, though a page-position ordinal isn't really "data" — a minor, debatable stretch, not a violation).
- Monospace/table discipline matches the live convention (`.mono-col`, `.fields dd`, `.detail`), same as the other two.
- Persona status renders via a new `.status-pill` class (rounded, colored) in the table's Status column — this has no direct precedent (live app has no persona-status pill styling confirmed), but it's scoped to one table column, not promoted into a page-wide badge system the way sidebar-glanceable's nav badges are, so it's a much smaller-radius deviation.

## Ranking (this lens only)

1. **minimal-jump-chips** — strongest. It is the only option that leaves the load-bearing conventions (panel shape, heading level, monospace scope, single `<details>` idiom, document order, panel visibility) completely untouched, and its one addition (a jump-chip bar) is the smallest, most isolated new element of the three. A viewer familiar with the live app would recognize this as the same product with a nav bar bolted on, not a different product.
2. **collapsible-clusters** — middle. It gets real credit for reusing the app's *actual* existing progressive-disclosure idiom (native `<details>`) rather than inventing a new one, which is a genuine point in its favor over sidebar-glanceable. But it pays for that by demoting panel headings from `<h2>` to `<h3>`, dropping the Personas panel's heading altogether, and adding a second, unrelated sticky-nav vocabulary on top — three separate, avoidable deviations from the shape this lens protects.
3. **sidebar-glanceable** — weakest. Its individual panels are the most faithful `<h2>`/`.panel-status` reproductions of the three, but the container around them — a persistent sidebar, single-active-panel switching, and a brand-new colored-pill status-badge system with no precedent anywhere in the current CSS — is the largest structural invention. This is the option most likely to be mistaken for a different, unrelated product (a settings/preferences app) rather than a restyled Mnemosyne, which is exactly the failure mode this lens is meant to catch.

## Strongest option through this lens alone

**minimal-jump-chips**, clearly. It is the only mockup where the load-bearing structural conventions — the per-panel `<section class="panel"><h2>…status…content</section>` shape, monospace-for-data-only, flat always-visible document order, and the app's sole existing `<details>` progressive-disclosure idiom — survive completely unmodified, and its only new element (the jump-chip bar) is additive, isolated, and reversible without touching anything else. collapsible-clusters is a reasonable second precisely because it reuses the same native-`<details>` idiom rather than inventing a new one, but it introduces avoidable heading-level and panel-identity regressions that minimal-jump-chips does not have. sidebar-glanceable, despite having the most faithful individual panel internals, invents the most new structural and visual vocabulary (hide/show panel switching, sidebar status badges) and is the one most at risk of reading as a different product on first glance.
