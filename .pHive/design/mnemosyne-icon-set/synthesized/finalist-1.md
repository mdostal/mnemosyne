# Finalist Recommendation 1: Ancient Urn (icon-08), refined — solid/thickened handles

## Derivation

Derives from **option 8, Ancient Urn / Vessel** (`icon-08-ancient-urn-vessel.jpg`), carried forward largely as-is, with one targeted construction change: **rebuild the two handles as thick, positive-space (filled) loops with a wide aperture, rather than the current thin negative-space cutouts near the shoulder.** Everything else about the mark — the tapered neck, swelling body, flared foot, single-fill silhouette, no surface ornamentation — should stay exactly as submitted.

Concretely: increase the handle stroke width to roughly match the vase's own wall thickness at the neck (currently the handle loop reads as a hairline-to-medium negative cut; it needs to read as a deliberately thick bent bar of the same material as the body), and open the negative-space hole inside each handle wider so it survives being pushed to a few pixels across at 16px without the hole closing up. This is a stroke-weight and aperture fix, not a re-concept — the amphora silhouette, proportions, and overall gesture are untouched.

## Why this option, grounded in the critiques

**Mythological/brand fit is the deciding factor, and Urn wins it outright.** The mythological-brand-fit lens ranks Ancient Urn #1 of 10, and says so in unusually strong terms: *"it's the only concept where 'Greek' and 'memory' are the same image rather than two separate asks"* and it's *"the only concept that combines an unambiguous Greek-antiquity silhouette... with a conceptual metaphor that actually fits the product: a vessel that holds something and can be drawn from later... a close functional match for what a memory/recall system does — capture and store, then retrieve."* No other option in the top tier of any other lens comes close on this axis: Owl is flagged in the same lens as actively **wrong** (*"the owl is Athena's animal, the symbol of wisdom, not Mnemosyne's... risks silently invoking the wrong goddess"*), and both Infinity Loop and Monogram M score near the bottom of that lens for having *"no mythological or antiquity signal whatsoever."* For a product that has staked its identity on a specific mythological name, getting that reference right is not a soft nice-to-have — the mythological-fit critique explicitly says the Urn is the one mark that would make a viewer "who knows the Mnemosyne reference nod in recognition."

**Every other lens converges on the exact same fixable weak point, which is what makes this a targeted refinement rather than a coin-flip pick.** Read across all six remaining lenses, and the Urn's flaw is named identically, every time, as the handles:

- Legibility-16px (rank 4/10, "middling-to-strong"): *"The risk is entirely in the two handles, which are drawn as thin negative-space loops... At 16px those loops are likely to close up... Unlike the spiral or the rings, this concept degrades to a simpler correct shape rather than to mud, which is the more forgivable failure mode."*
- Dark-UI-context fit (rank 3/10): *"even the handles are thick enough to survive scaling down. No hairlines, no small cutouts... one dependable amber mass."* (Already closer to safe here — but this lens is judging the handles as drawn, and still flags them as the one place to watch.)
- Tab-bar distinctiveness (rank 5/10): *"The handle cutouts are thin and will likely fuse into the body outline at 16px, softening the 'vase' read... but the overall silhouette should still stand apart from neighboring tabs."*
- Accessibility/contrast (rank 5/10): *"The two handle loops are cut as negative-space holes; they're moderately sized (not hairline) and should survive typical favicon scaling better than the laurel's or scroll's fine details, though they are still the first thing to watch."*
- Multi-resolution scalability (rank 5/10): *"The handles are the one soft spot: each is a moderately narrow open loop, and at favicon size those apertures... can fill in, turning the handles into two solid bumps rather than open loops. That's a real but fairly forgiving failure mode — the urn still reads as 'vessel' even if the handles solidify."*
- Visual-language cohesion (rank 8/10, weakest showing): flags the urn as "naturalistic... closer to clip-art of a vase than an abstracted mark" — this is the one lens where thickening the handles doesn't fully solve the problem, but the same critique also credits the execution as *"a single solid silhouette with no surface ornamentation... clean, single-technique,"* meaning the cohesion issue is about overall contour complexity (lip/neck/shoulder/belly/foot), not primarily the handles — a genuine tradeoff to name honestly, not paper over.

Six of seven lenses independently point at the same specific, small, geometric detail as the mark's only real liability, and five of them explicitly describe its failure mode as "graceful" or "forgivable" (degrades to a simpler correct silhouette, not to mud, unlike the spiral or rings which fail catastrophically). That is close to an ideal profile for a refinement brief: fix one clearly-identified construction weakness and the mark should climb meaningfully on legibility-16px, tab-bar distinctiveness, accessibility, and multi-resolution scalability simultaneously, without touching the property that makes it uniquely strong (mythological fit) or the one property (dark-UI solidity) that's already fine.

## Honest tradeoff

This pick is not the technically safest option in the set — Owl and Infinity Loop outscore it on raw shape-survivability lenses (legibility, scalability, tab-bar pop) and would need no rework at all. Visual-language cohesion also won't fully resolve with the handle fix alone, since that critique's complaint is partly about the vase's naturalistic multi-curve contour, not only the handles. The case for the Urn over those technically cleaner options is that it is the only concept in the entire set where the mythological-brand-fit lens and the memory-function metaphor point at the same image — a distinction none of the other technically stronger candidates can claim, and one worth a small, well-scoped construction fix to capture.
